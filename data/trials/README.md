# Trial texts

Long public-domain texts that `tonika trial` runs through the whole pipeline, to prove that
Tonika changes nothing but pronunciation marks and to record speed, memory and review load.
They are not labelled for accuracy. Results: `docs/TRIALS.md`.

**Why it is public domain.** Chekhov died in 1904, more than 70 years ago, so his works are in
the public domain in Russia and in every country with a term of life plus 70 years or less. The
story was first published in 1899, before 1931, so it is also in the public domain in the
United States. A plain transcription of a public-domain text creates no new copyright.

**What was taken.** Wikisource reproduces the text of the academic edition (Полное собрание
сочинений и писем в 30 томах, Сочинения, т. 10, pp. 128–143), in modern orthography. Only the
author's words were kept. Removed: the page header and its bibliographic note, the eight
Wikisource footnotes (`Примечание ВТ`) and their anchors, the notes section, categories and
interlanguage links. No commentary, footnote or editorial note of that edition or of Wikisource
is in the file.

**What was changed.** Nothing in the author's text. Chapter headings `== I ==` became the
Roman numeral on its own line; the italics around the letter «ъ» were dropped, the letter kept.
Paragraphs are separated by one blank line. The file is UTF-8 in NFC and keeps the source's
spelling exactly: ё where the edition prints it («всё», «шёпотом»), е elsewhere («ее», «еще»),
and the non-breaking space the source puts before a dash. The source carries no stress marks.
